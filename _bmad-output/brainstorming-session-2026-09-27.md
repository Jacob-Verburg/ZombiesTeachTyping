## Brainstorming Approach

**Selected Mode:** Guided — facilitator walks through techniques in sequence

**Seed Concept:** "Mario Teaches Typing" meets zombies. A typing-tutor game for kids ages 6–13, inspired by the October/Halloween season. Tone: goofy (Plants vs. Zombies), not scary (House of the Dead).

**Technique Sequence (revised after Party Mode roundtable):**
1. **Toy Before Game** — Find the juicy keypress before anything else
2. **Player Fantasy Mining** — Who is the kid in this world? Does the fantasy shift with age?
3. **Verbs Before Nouns / Core Loop Brainstorming** — What happens on every keypress? Why press the next one?
4. **Progression Curve Sculpting & Flow Analysis** — The curriculum is the foundation (home row → words → sentences)
5. **Age-Range & Accessibility Layers** — One scaling game or distinct modes for 6-year-olds vs. 13-year-olds?
6. **Genre Mashup & Reference Blending** — Typing tutor × zombie genres; Mario Teaches Typing, Typing of the Dead, Epistory, Z-Type, Plants vs. Zombies
7. **Failure State Design** — Kid-friendly, funny, instructive failure
8. **Emotion Targeting & Moment Design** — Goofy-spooky peak moments
9. **Ludonarrative Harmony / Environmental Storytelling** — The world and why zombies care about typing
10. **Wild Round** — Remix an Existing Game, Anti-Game Design, What If Scenarios, Constraint Box

**Focus Areas:**
- Core gameplay loop tied to real typing skill (home row → words → sentences)
- Player fantasy and a mascot/hero with Mario-style charm
- Tone calibration: goofy and silly, never truly scary, for ages 6–13
- Progression and difficulty scaling across a wide age range
- Learning design: accuracy vs. speed, finger placement, feedback
- Reward and meta-progression systems that motivate kids
- Seasonal/Halloween flavor, world, and narrative
- Parent/teacher considerations

### Tone, Feel & Core Principles

**[Tone #1]**: Goofy, Not Scary
_Core Loop_: Zombies are cartoonish and silly, in the Plants vs. Zombies style, never House of the Dead horror.
_Novelty_: A zombie typing game that is safe and inviting for 6-year-olds.

**[Feel #17]**: 90s Sprite Simplicity
_Core Loop_: The zombie is a clean, classic sprite with simple walk/hop/collect animations and no body-part gags.
_Novelty_: Deliberately retro restraint, like the original Mario Teaches Typing, instead of modern over-juiced feedback.

**[Tone #20]**: Goofy, Never Babyish
_Core Loop_: No silly songs or toddler-style gags; humor must work for both a 6-year-old and a 13-year-old.
_Novelty_: A tone guardrail that keeps older kids engaged.

**[Principle #36]**: Short-Burst Practice
_Core Loop_: Each session is 5–15 minutes of casual typing practice, with no ongoing storyline and no RPG progression.
_Novelty_: Designed around real kid schedules (after school, before dinner, classroom downtime).

**[Principle #38]**: Typing Is the Only Skill
_Core Loop_: No lane choices and no strategy decisions; the only thing asked of the player is to type well.
_Novelty_: Every bit of the player's attention goes to the learning goal.

**[Principle #43]**: Skill ≠ Age
_Core Loop_: Never assume ability from age. A fast 8-year-old and a beginner 13-year-old both get the right challenge.
_Novelty_: Avoids "baby mode" stigma; older beginners are never labeled.

**[Principle #45]**: Remove Choices, Keep It Simple
_Core Loop_: Wherever the game can decide something sensibly, it does. The kid just picks a level and types.
_Novelty_: Fewer menus mean more minutes spent practicing.

**[Principle #50]**: Invisible Difficulty
_Core Loop_: The kid never sees a rank, tier or "difficulty went down" message; the game silently adjusts.
_Novelty_: No shame spiral; a bad day never becomes a public demotion.

**[Principle #51]**: Sibling-Proof Design
_Core Loop_: The rolling 5-game average absorbs outlier runs (a bad day, a sibling mashing keys) without penalizing anyone.
_Novelty_: Designed for shared family computers.

**[Principle #80]**: Art Variety, Mechanical Consistency
_Core Loop_: Variety comes from art, never from rule changes within a level.
_Novelty_: Kids always know how to play, yet it feels fresh; solo-dev friendly scope.

**[Principle #84]**: Plain-Word Vocabulary
_Core Loop_: All UI text, names and low-difficulty word lists use words a 6-year-old recognizes; zombie slang stays out of menus.
_Novelty_: Accessibility for early readers.

**[Principle #96]**: Scope Discipline
_Core Loop_: If it's nice but not needed, park it. New modes, levels or systems are out for v1.
_Novelty_: Protects the game's core strength: small, clear, finishable.

### Structure & Menus

**[Structure #2]**: Level-Select Menu
_Core Loop_: A Mario Teaches Typing–style menu where the player picks a level.
_Novelty_: Familiar, simple structure proven by the genre's classic.

**[Structure #3]**: Each Level Has a Twist
_Core Loop_: Every level applies a different gameplay gimmick to the same typing curriculum.
_Novelty_: Each level feels like its own mini-game.

**[Structure #28]**: Three Levels, Three Gimmicks
_Core Loop_: Exactly 3 levels, as in MTT: Zombie Run, Horde Rush, Pitchfork Panic.
_Novelty_: Tight, finishable scope.

**[Structure #37]**: Standalone Levels
_Core Loop_: Levels are self-contained and playable in any order, any time. No gating.
_Novelty_: Kids jump straight to the practice they want.

**[Structure #48]**: Levels Are Replayable Lessons
_Core Loop_: Every level can be replayed endlessly; each is a typing lesson with a game layer.
_Novelty_: Replay is practice, not grinding.

**[Emotion #35]**: Collect → Attack → Escape
_Core Loop_: The three levels move from calm gathering, to going on the offensive, to a desperate escape.
_Novelty_: A different emotional register per level within one theme.

**[Naming #81]**: Horde Rush (Level 2)
**[Naming #82]**: Pitchfork Panic (Level 3)

**[Emotion #83]**: Calm & Cheerful Warm-Up
_Core Loop_: Relaxed, sunny mood; a gentle stroll before the two high-pressure levels.
_Novelty_: Starts the calm → rush → panic intensity curve.

**[Level 1 #5]**: Brain Blocks
_Core Loop_: MTT's letter blocks in the sky become cartoon brain blocks carrying letters.
_Novelty_: The collectible is also the typing target.

**[Level 1 #6]**: Villagers Carry Letters

_Novelty_: Humans become the targets, keeping the role reversal.

**[Level 1 #9]**: Zombified, Not Eaten
_Core Loop_: Typing a villager's letter makes the zombie hug them and they pop into a party-hat zombie.
_Novelty_: Kid-safe "conversion" instead of violence.

**[Level 1 #10]**: Zombie Conga Line
_Core Loop_: Zombified villagers follow behind the player in a growing line.
_Novelty_: A visible streak/progress meter.

**[Level 1 #12]**: Brain Bonk

_Core Loop_: Zombie Run lasts 2 minutes; collect as much as possible.
_Novelty_: Snackable sessions.

**[Economy #47]**: Skill-Proportional Rewards
_Core Loop_: Faster, more accurate typing naturally collects more brains and villagers in the same 2 minutes.

**[Art #77]**: Randomized Backdrops

### Level 2: Horde Rush

**[Level 2 #29]**: Word Spawner
_Core Loop_: Words appear at the bottom of the screen; each completed word spawns a copy of your zombie that walks down a lane toward a defended house.
_Novelty_: Plants vs. Zombies with roles reversed; typing speed is your army.

**[Level 2 #30]**: The Pacing Defender
_Core Loop_: A single human runs back and forth across the lanes, firing a cartoon bullet down any lane with a zombie as they pass.
_Novelty_: One simple AI creates real tension. The defender builds nothing.

**[Level 2 #31]**: Speed = Victory
_Core Loop_: Slow typing lets the defender pick zombies off; fast typing overwhelms them and gets zombies to the house.
_Novelty_: WPM becomes visible army strength.

**[Level 2 #40]**: Random Lane Spawns
_Core Loop_: Each zombie spawns in a random lane.
_Novelty_: A varied battlefield with zero player choice.

**[Level 2 #41]**: Word Length = Zombie Size
_Core Loop_: Short words spawn small, fast zombies; long words spawn big brutes that take more hits.
_Novelty_: Visual variety and a hidden reward for harder words.

**[Level 2 #54]**: Red Flash Damage
_Core Loop_: A zombie's sprite flashes red when hit.
_Novelty_: Classic 90s, readable, non-gory feedback.

**[Level 2 #55]**: Melt-Away Defeat
_Core Loop_: After enough hits, the zombie falls over and shrinks vertically, melting into the ground.
_Novelty_: Cartoon defeat, nothing violent.

**[Level 2 #56]**: 5-Minute Siege
_Core Loop_: A 5-minute timer; each zombie reaching the house earns one brain.
_Novelty_: WPM converts directly into currency.

**[Art #78]**: Rotating House + Defender Pairs
_Core Loop_: About 3 house styles, each with its own defender, chosen at random each play.
_Novelty_: A fresh-looking siege with no new mechanics.

### Level 3: Pitchfork Panic

**[Level 3 #32]**: Mob Chase
_Core Loop_: A paragraph sits on the side of the screen; each typed word moves the zombie forward, away from a torch-and-pitchfork mob.
_Novelty_: The classic horror chase reversed: the monster flees.

**[Level 3 #33]**: Accelerating Mob
_Core Loop_: The mob starts slow and speeds up; survive as long as you can.
_Novelty_: Endless survival with a built-in skill ceiling.

**[Level 3 #57]**: Randomized Brain Pickups
_Core Loop_: While surviving, a brain appears on the path every 5–30 seconds (random interval).
_Novelty_: Rewards survival time with small surprises.

**[Level 3 #58]**: Caught → Report Card
_Core Loop_: When the mob catches the zombie, the run ends straight into the score screen.
_Novelty_: Quick, clean failure; no drawn-out game over.

**[Art #79]**: Random Chase Settings
_Core Loop_: About 3 themed chase environments, chosen at random.
_Novelty_: Discovery instead of repetition.

### Input, Failure & Feedback

**[Mechanic #22]**: Blocking Input
_Core Loop_: A wrong key does nothing: no progress, no collection, until the correct key is pressed.
_Novelty_: Frustration-free, accuracy-focused, true to MTT.

**[UX #39]**: Consistent Word Position
_Core Loop_: The target letter or word always sits in the same place at the bottom of the screen in every level.
_Novelty_: Eyes learn one spot; less searching, more typing.

**[HUD #8]**: Shared HUD
_Core Loop_: Every level shows timer, typed count, WPM, errors and target letter/word in the bottom bar.
_Novelty_: One reusable, familiar interface.

**[Feedback #59]**: Unified Chalkboard Report Card
_Core Loop_: All 3 levels end on one score screen: Keys Typed, Errors, WPM, Accuracy, Lesson Time, Brains Collected, with NEXT and MENU buttons. (Merges idea #24.)
_Novelty_: One consistent "lesson complete" moment.

**[Art #60]**: Professor Zombie
_Core Loop_: The report card shows the player's zombie in a graduation cap and gown pointing at a chalkboard, wearing their cosmetics.
_Novelty_: A nod to Professor Mario that shows off earned gear.

### Learning Design & Curriculum

_Core Loop_: Green zombie hands replace MTT's white gloves in the HUD.

_Core Loop_: The hands light up the correct finger for the next key in all 3 levels.

**[Learning #87]**: Next-Letter Finger Tracking
_Core Loop_: In words and paragraphs, the hands light the finger for the next letter, moving along as the kid types.

_Core Loop_: For capitals in Level 3, the hands light both the opposite pinky on Shift and the letter's finger.

_Core Loop_: Easiest difficulty uses home-row keys only; top row, then bottom row, are added as skill rises. Applies to all 3 levels.

**[Curriculum #89]**: Row-Filtered Word Lists
_Core Loop_: At low difficulty, words use only unlocked rows (e.g. "dad", "sad", "ask").

**[Curriculum #91]**: Real Writing in Pitchfork Panic

## Themes and Patterns

1. **90s simplicity:** classic sprites, restrained audio, the proven Mario Teaches Typing structure (level menu, finger-guide hands, chalkboard report card).
2. **Kid-first respect:** goofy but not babyish, plain words, sibling-proof, invisible difficulty, no shame.
3. **Typing is the only skill:** no strategy, no lane picking, blocking input; all challenge comes from typing itself.
4. **Variety through art, never through rules:** randomized backdrops, houses/defenders and chase settings.
5. **Curriculum built into the structure:** letters → words → paragraphs across levels; home → top → bottom rows across difficulty.
6. **Scope discipline:** short-burst practice sessions, 3 levels, 18 cosmetics, solo-dev finishable.

## Promising Combinations

- **Placement test + row scoping + word length (#93, #94, #88, #49):** a complete, invisible adaptive curriculum from one rolling WPM number.
- **Welcome bonus + first-purchase tutorial + pets in the HUD (#68, #64, #63):** onboarding that teaches the economy and immediately puts a companion on screen during typing.
- **Calm → Rush → Panic (#83, #35, #34):** emotional intensity rises in step with typing complexity (letters → words → paragraphs).
- **Professor Zombie + visible cosmetics (#60, #16):** every report card becomes a moment to show off earned gear.
- **Shared HUD + consistent word position + finger guide (#8, #39, #86):** one reusable interface that makes every level instantly familiar.

## Session Summary

### Most Promising Concepts

**Top Pick: Horde Rush — Reverse Plants vs. Zombies Typing Siege**
The most novel idea of the session. Each typed word spawns a zombie (bigger words mean bigger zombies) that marches on a house guarded by a single pacing defender. WPM becomes a visible army, so kids *see* their typing speed as power. It is easy to read for a 6-year-old and still tense for a 13-year-old, and its mechanics are simple (random lanes, one defender AI, one timer).

**Runner-up: The Invisible Adaptive Curriculum**
A full-keyboard placement run, then a rolling 5-game WPM average that silently scopes keyboard rows (home → top → bottom) and word length across all three levels. It solves the widest design problem, a 6–13 audience where skill doesn't match age, with no menus, no labels and no shame. It is also sibling-proof.

**Honorable Mention: Zombie Run + Crypt Closet Onboarding**
The calm, MTT-faithful first level (brain blocks, hug-to-zombify villagers, conga line) doubles as the placement test. The welcome bonus leads straight into the Crypt Closet, where the first hat and pet teach the brain economy, and the pet then keeps the kid company in the HUD.

### Key Insights

1. **Role reversal is the hook.** Being the (goofy) zombie sets this apart from every "shoot the zombie" typing game.
2. **One reusable frame, three gimmicks.** The shared HUD, fixed word position, finger-guide hands and chalkboard report card mean each level adds only its own gimmick, which keeps the scope realistic for a solo developer.
3. **The curriculum lives in the structure.** Letters → words → paragraphs across levels, and home → top → bottom rows across difficulty.
4. **Variety comes from art, not rules.** About 3 randomized backdrops/settings per level keep replays fresh without new mechanics.
5. **Respect the kid.** Goofy but not babyish, plain words, blocking input instead of penalties, and difficulty never shown.
6. **Scope discipline is a feature.** 3 levels, 18 cosmetics, short-burst sessions; extras are parked, not lost.

### Open Questions

- Should "Zombie Run" (the calm level) and "Pitchfork Panic" (the literal chase) be renamed to avoid confusion?
- The exact WPM → difficulty mapping (row unlock thresholds, word-length bands per level).
- Brain payout tuning per level against the 100/200/300 shop tiers, and the welcome bonus amount.
- The remaining Crypt Closet slots (4–7 hats, 1–4 pets unconfirmed).
- The Level 3 "caught" moment: animation before the report card?
- The 3 art themes for each level (backdrops, house/defender pairs, chase settings).

### Recommended Next Steps

1. **Create a Game Brief** (`gds-create-game-brief`) using this session as input: lock the pillars (role reversal, invisible adaptive curriculum, 90s simplicity, scope discipline).
2. **Prototype Zombie Run in Godot first.** It is closest to MTT and builds the shared foundation every level reuses: keyboard input with blocking, HUD and finger-guide hands, WPM/accuracy tracking, and the report card.
3. **Prototype Horde Rush next** to test the top-pick fantasy: does watching your words become an army feel good, and does a single pacing defender create enough tension?
